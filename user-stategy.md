# Tour-op: 30-day user acquisition plan

14 October – 12 November 2026 · Ho Chi Minh City · Organic acquisition · Website + Android testing

My recommendation is to launch a founder-led pilot for personalized local experiences in Ho Chi Minh City, rather than a broad marketing campaign.

You will recruit both travelers and local providers, but you should not spend equal effort on both every week. First, assemble a small group of suitable providers; then bring travelers to them and manually facilitate the first matches. This is the most practical way to tackle the two-sided marketplace cold-start problem without an advertising budget.&#x20;

### Where to source provider leads

- Google Maps: search for walking tours, food tours, cultural experiences and independent tour operators in Ho Chi Minh City.
- Instagram and TikTok: find local experience hosts who regularly publish their activities.
- LinkedIn: identify freelance guides, tourism professionals and small tour-company owners.
- Facebook: look for local tourism, tour-guide and travel-business communities.
- Personal network: ask friends, former colleagues and acquaintances whether they know anyone working in tourism.

Create a spreadsheet with the provider's name, source, contact channel, experience type, last contact date, response, next step and profile status. Use publicly available business contact details appropriately, and respect group rules and opt-out requests.

### Provider outreach script

Hi [Name]! I came across your [tour/experience] in Ho Chi Minh City and wanted to reach out.

I'm building Tour-op, a platform that helps travelers discover local people and personalized experiences that match their interests.

I'm currently inviting a small group of local experience providers to test the platform. I'd also love to understand how you currently attract customers and what makes it difficult to get more bookings.

Would you be open to a quick 10-minute chat? I'm happy to help you explore the platform and set up your profile if it's a good fit.

Website: [https://tour-op.com/](https://tour-op.com/)

Important: Your initial ask is a conversation, not a commitment. A provider who tells you why they would not use Tour-op is more valuable than a registration that never becomes active.

## Week 2 — Complete the initial supply and activate travelers

21–27 October

Objective: Reach 10–12 complete provider profiles and acquire your first 15–20 qualified traveler leads.

### Actions

- Contact another 25–30 providers.
- Onboard providers personally instead of asking everyone to figure out the product alone.
- Invite 10 people from your personal network who have traveled in Vietnam or regularly explore HCMC.
- Join relevant travel communities and contribute useful advice before sharing a testing invitation.
- Ask every provider you onboard to introduce one potential traveler or another suitable provider.
- Help the first interested travelers identify an experience that fits their preferences.

### Traveler interview questions

Ask five to ten people questions such as:

1. How do you currently find activities or local guides in a new city?
2. What was frustrating about the last time you booked an experience?
3. What information do you need before trusting a local guide?
4. Would you prefer browsing profiles or submitting your interests and receiving recommendations?
5. What would make you comfortable booking through a new platform?

Avoid leading questions such as “Would you use my app?” People may be polite without having any intention of using it.

### Traveler outreach script

Hey! I'm testing Tour-op, a new platform I'm building to help travelers connect with local people and find experiences that fit their interests.

I'm starting in Ho Chi Minh City and looking for a few early travelers to try it out. I'd love to know how you normally discover local experiences and what would make the process easier.

Would you be interested in trying it and giving me honest feedback?

[https://tour-op.com/](https://tour-op.com/)

## Week 3 — Run the first real matching experiments

28 October – 3 November

Objective: Get the first 3–5 successful traveler-provider introductions.

This is where you stop thinking primarily about sign-ups and start operating Tour-op as a service.

1\. Collect traveler requests

Ask about travel dates, interests, group size, approximate budget, preferred language and any relevant accessibility needs.

2\. Manually shortlist suitable providers

Contact providers to confirm availability, experience details and pricing. Do not rely on stale listings or assume someone is available.

3\. Make the introduction

Introduce the traveler to one or two suitable providers through the platform or another consented channel supported by your current workflow.

4\. Follow up after the introduction

Ask whether the provider responded, whether the experience matched expectations, and whether the traveler booked or completed it.

Manual matching is not a weakness at this stage. It lets you learn which interests, prices, availability and trust signals actually drive successful matches before investing time in more automation.&#x20;

### Content to publish this week

Publish three useful posts on your personal LinkedIn and, where relevant, in communities that allow them:

- “What makes a local travel experience worth paying for?”

## Week 4 — Test referrals and willingness to pay

4–12 November

Objective: Reach 15 complete provider profiles, 60 cumulative traveler leads, and 5–10 successful introductions or bookings.

### Actions

1. Follow up with every interested person who has not completed onboarding.
2. Ask active providers to refer one other suitable provider.
3. Ask satisfied travelers whether they would recommend Tour-op to a friend.
4. Conduct five short interviews with users who did not convert.
5. Test one revenue hypothesis with a small number of providers and customers.
6. Review which channel generated the most active users, not merely replies.

By the end of the month, you should have enough evidence to decide whether to concentrate on provider recruitment, traveler acquisition, product usability or trust and safety.

## 11. Revenue experiments: validate before monetizing

I would not introduce a mandatory subscription or large commission before Tour-op has demonstrated that it can generate value. Instead, run small, transparent experiments.

Experiment A — Free founding-provider cohort

Start here

Invite 5–10 suitable providers to join the pilot for free. Help them create quality profiles and seek real traveler inquiries.

Validate: Will providers invest time in their profiles and respond promptly even before you have substantial traffic?

Cost: Your time.

Experiment B — Commission on completed bookings

Once you can track bookings and help both sides complete them, test a modest commission, for example 5–10%, with a few willing providers.

Validate: Are providers willing to pay for incremental bookings, and does the resulting fee justify your operational work?

Do not charge a commission on bookings you cannot reliably attribute to Tour-op or before clearly agreeing on the terms.

Experiment C — Paid promotion for providers

Ask two or three providers whether they would pay for additional visibility or qualified inquiries once the platform has relevant traveler traffic.

Validate: Is there a valuable outcome providers would pay for repeatedly?

You can test willingness to pay through interviews first. Do not build a premium feature until the demand is demonstrated.

My preferred order is A → B → C. Free onboarding removes friction while you build supply; a booking commission tests whether Tour-op creates measurable economic value; paid promotion only makes sense when you can demonstrate a credible audience.

## 13. What to do when the numbers are weak

Many messages, few replies

Narrow the prospect list, personalize the opening line and ask for a short conversation rather than a sign-up.

Providers join but don't respond

Talk to them about the value they expect, make onboarding easier and show how inquiries will reach them.

Travelers register but don't submit requests

Clarify the next step, reduce unnecessary fields and test whether they understand the value before registration.

Travelers request experiences but cannot find a match

Narrow the experience categories, confirm provider availability manually and recruit supply that matches actual requests.

Matches happen but nobody pays

Interview both sides about pricing, trust, perceived value and the reason they did not book. Do not assume the solution is a lower price.

## 14. Your first action today

Start with three things:

1. Build a list of 30 HCMC providers who offer experiences that Tour-op can genuinely support.
2. Contact five of them personally using the provider script above.
3. Invite three potential travelers to discuss how they discover local experiences, then record what they actually need.

Do not spend the first week redesigning the entire website or building more features unless testing reveals a genuine obstacle to onboarding or matching.

The goal for November 12 is not to prove that Tour-op can attract a lot of traffic. It is to prove that you can repeatedly find a suitable provider, find a traveler who needs that experience, and bring them together successfully. That is the foundation for both organic growth and future revenue.
