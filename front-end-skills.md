# Tour-op UI/UX redesign: from testing to investor readiness

I would make one important strategic decision before changing the frontend:

Tour-op should sell the feeling of discovering local experiences, while proving that the people behind those experiences can be trusted.

Your focus on profiles, safety and verification is not a mistake. Those are important foundations for a two-sided marketplace. The issue is how to communicate them without overwhelming a first-time visitor before they understand what Tour-op offers.

I could open [tour-op.com](https://tour-op.com/), but the available page view did not expose its rendered content, so I can't honestly claim to have audited your exact current layout, component hierarchy or CSS. The recommendations below combine your user feedback, your product priorities, and patterns visible on competing local-experience websites. For example, Withlocals leads with local hosts and personalized experiences, while other Vietnam-focused platforms combine strong imagery with discoverable experience categories and pricing.&#x20;

# 4. Currency and pricing UX

Your feedback about USD is especially important for international travelers.

I recommend a small currency selector in the header or in the experience discovery controls.

### Proposed price display

# $25.00

Price per person · 2-hour experience

This is a UI example, not a live exchange-rate calculator. The selector demonstrates the interaction only.

For the real implementation:

- Store the provider's actual price and currency, rather than storing only a converted display value.
- Convert for display using a reliable exchange-rate source, or a clearly disclosed fixed indicative rate if you cannot afford a live service.
- Label converted prices as estimates and keep the original price accessible.
- Make the pricing basis explicit: per person, per group, per hour or per activity.
- If the provider has not set a price, display “Request a quote” instead of inventing a number.
- Don't imply that Tour-op processes payments or guarantees a booking if it does not.

Initially, you do not need a complicated financial infrastructure. A simple selector and a trustworthy pricing presentation are enough to test whether this feedback improves user understanding.

# 5. Step-by-step roadmap when you have almost no money

Do not try to redesign everything simultaneously. I would divide the work into four stages, each with a clear objective and exit criteria.

## Stage 1 — Remove confusion

Now

Week 1 · Cost: primarily your time

Priority: P0

- Rewrite the hero headline and subheadline.
- Explain what locals offer using three or four real categories.
- Separate “I'm traveling” and “I'm a local” actions.
- Clarify what happens after a user clicks the main button.
- Add USD/VND display support if your pricing data permits it.
- Fix mobile layout issues, broken links, unclear labels and loading errors.

Exit criteria: Five new testers can explain Tour-op's purpose and identify their next step without your help.

## Stage 2 — Improve visual discovery

Weeks 2–3

Cost: low if you reuse existing assets

Priority: P1

- Add one strong hero image or a small manual carousel.
- Add swipeable experience cards using real provider images.
- Improve card hierarchy and progressive disclosure.
- Add concise explanations and clear price states.
- Optimize images and test performance on mobile networks.

Exit criteria: Users can find a relevant experience and understand its price status without assistance.

## Stage 3 — Build trust into the journey

Weeks 3–4

Cost: mainly implementation and verification operations

Priority: P1

- Improve provider profile completeness.
- Explain verification status and what it covers.
- Make reviews, contact rules and reporting easy to find.
- Explain the process from inquiry to confirmed experience.
- Measure where users abandon onboarding or matching.

Exit criteria: You can explain how a traveler evaluates a provider and what happens if something goes wrong.

## Stage 4 — Prove traction and prepare to raise

After initial pilot

Cost: use actual traction to justify further investment

Priority: P2

- Improve the highest-friction user journey based on observed data.
- Introduce better search, filters or matching only where needed.
- Collect permission-based user testimonials and real usage evidence.
- Test provider willingness to pay.
- Prepare a concise investor deck and a specific funding request.

Exit criteria: You have evidence of a repeatable way to acquire users and help travelers find suitable providers.

These stages can overlap. For example, you should fix a serious safety or privacy defect immediately rather than waiting until Stage 3.

## 7. How to make the slideshow effective without paying for design

You don't need dozens of professional images or a complex animation library.

I would start with:

- One strong hero image that shows a real local experience.
- Four category images that match the activities actually available.
- Three to six featured experience images from consenting providers.
- A consistent aspect ratio, sensible image compression and descriptive alt text.

Use genuine provider photography wherever possible. If you use illustrative stock or generated images, don't present them as evidence of a specific provider's actual experience.

For mobile, a swipeable carousel with visible controls is usually a better starting point than a full-screen auto-playing slider. Give users direct access to the next card and keep the main action visible.

The goal is not to maximize the number of pictures. It is to help users understand what they can do.

# 8. How to validate the redesign before coding everything

Run a small usability experiment with five travelers and five potential providers.

## Usability test checklist

Mark each test as completed when you have observed a real person performing the task.

Show the homepage for five seconds, then ask what Tour-op does.

Ask the tester to find a relevant local experience.

Ask the tester to identify the price, currency and pricing basis.

Ask the tester to assess a provider's profile and verification status.

Ask the tester to explain what they would do next and what makes them hesitate.

Completion: 0 of 5 tests

Record the number of people who complete each task without help. Note their exact objections, and distinguish what they say from what they actually do.

Then compare the existing page with the updated page using the same tasks. You do not need a large analytics budget to identify obvious confusion.

# 9. What investors will want to see

The redesign should support the business, not become the business itself.

An investor will generally want evidence that Tour-op solves a real problem, can acquire users efficiently, can create successful matches and has a plausible route to revenue. A beautiful homepage alone is not evidence of product-market fit.

Prepare these five items as you progress:

1\. Evidence of demand

How many travelers express genuine interest, submit requests and return?

2\. Quality of supply

How many active providers have complete profiles, real offers and confirmed availability?

3\. Matching performance

What proportion of qualified traveler requests receive a suitable response or successful introduction?

4\. Revenue hypothesis

Who pays, what do they pay for, and what evidence supports that willingness to pay?

5\. Repeatable acquisition

Which organic channels produce active travelers and providers, and how much founder time does each require?

For an investor-facing demo, I would show a traveler discovering an experience, inspecting the local's profile and verification details, understanding the price, and proceeding to a clear next step. Then show the provider side: creating a profile, listing an offer and receiving an inquiry. Use real functionality rather than mock metrics.

## 10. What I would not spend money on yet

- A complete visual rebrand.
- A paid design agency.
- An expensive image library.
- A complicated recommendation engine.
- Paid user acquisition before you understand conversion.
- A large verification operation without a clear risk-based process.
- More features simply because competitors have them.

Instead, spend your limited resources on a clear value proposition, a usable mobile experience, credible local profiles, transparent pricing and a functioning path from discovery to contact.

## My final recommendation

Your next move should be a small, measurable frontend improvement release, not a complete redesign.

1. Clarify the homepage message and the two user journeys.
2. Fix pricing and currency presentation.
3. Add curated experience imagery and improve the card layout.
4. Preserve and improve your profile, verification and safety features.
5. Test the new experience with real people.
6. Use the results to decide what to build next and what to show investors.

One final point: because Tour-op is a marketplace, you need both compelling discovery and credible trust. If you focus exclusively on trust, users may not understand why they should explore the platform. If you focus exclusively on beautiful images, users may not trust the people they discover. The design should connect those two strengths.
